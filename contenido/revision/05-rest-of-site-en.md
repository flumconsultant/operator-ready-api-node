## Cómo transformamos — `/en/how-we-transform`

BECOME TRANSFORMATION FRAMEWORK

# Transformation happens inside.

We do not add AI on top of the company. We redesign, from the inside, the five systems that decide whether the change stays installed or dies with the pilot.

THE THESIS

## Six stages and five systems.

Most AI initiatives do not fail because of the model. They fail because they were built next to the real work: the process stayed the same, nobody owned the outcome, and there was no measure against which to decide whether it was worth keeping.

This method exists to give that redesign an order. These are not consulting phases invented to justify a calendar: it is the sequence in which the decisions depend on each other. You cannot choose a capability before you know where the value is, and you cannot measure what was never defined.

THE FIVE SYSTEMS

## What changes together, not in parallel.

These are not five simultaneous projects. Every stage takes decisions across all five, because leaving one out is precisely what makes the change fail to survive its first quarter.

### People

People able to work, decide and collaborate with AI: with their own judgment about what a model can and cannot be asked for, and with ownership of the outcome.

### Data

Structured and unstructured data prepared to feed models, retrieval and decisions, with permissions and traceability defined before it is used.

### Agents

Models, copilots and agents able to reason, use tools and execute parts of a workflow within explicit limits.

### Products

Products and experiences that embed models, data and agents in the value proposition, not only in the internal process.

### Operations

Workflows redesigned to combine automation, agents, human decisions, exceptions and controls in a single process.

THE SIX STAGES

## From ambition to value, in the only order that works.

01

### Frame the outcome

- — Which business outcome has to change, and by when?
- — Who answers for that outcome today?
- — How will we know it changed, without arguing about it?

Output: A declared outcome, with an owner and an agreed measure set before anything starts.

02

### Understand the system

- — How does the process actually work today, exceptions included?
- — Where do the knowledge, the data and the decisions live?
- — What limits the change: the technology, the data, the permission or the habit?

Output: A diagnosis of the real process and of readiness across the five systems.

03

### Find the value

- — Where does AI create differential value rather than merely visible value?
- — What comes first, what waits, and why?
- — What does it cost not to do it?

Output: A portfolio prioritised by value, feasibility, speed and risk.

04

### Choose the capability

- — Which specific capability solves the prioritised problem?
- — What does a person decide, and what does a system not decide?
- — Which controls and which evidence are required to operate it?

Output: The target operating model and the solution design, with its limits written down.

05

### Build & embed

- — How is it built inside the real work rather than beside it?
- — Who uses it every day, and what do they need in order to trust it?
- — What happens when it fails, and who resolves it?

Output: The capability running inside the operation, with oversight and exceptions defined.

06

### Measure & evolve

- — What changed in the business, against the agreed baseline?
- — Is it being used, and with what level of confidence?
- — Do we iterate, integrate, scale or stop?

Output: A scorecard and a scale decision taken on evidence, with a real option to stop.

HOW WE WORK

## Five principles we do not trade for speed.

### Value before technology

The conversation does not start with which model to use. It starts with which decision, which process or which experience has to change. The technology is chosen afterwards — which is why it can be changed without redoing the strategy.

### Build with, not for

The team that will operate the capability helps design it. Not for consensus: because the people who run a process are the ones who know its exceptions, and those exceptions decide whether something works in real life.

### Adoption by design

Adoption is not a communication plan at the end. It is a design decision from the start: what gets embedded in the tool people already use, what gets simplified, and what stops being asked of people at all.

### Governance from the start

Limits, human oversight, traceability and accountability are defined before anything is built. Adding them later means rebuilding — and in a supervised environment, rebuilding with an auditor watching.

### Ownership stays inside

When the work ends, the company holds the judgment, the documentation and the people able to operate and evolve what was built. If continuing requires calling us again, the work is not finished.

## IA responsable — `/en/about/responsible-ai`

RESPONSIBLE AI

# Controls get designed with the solution, not bolted on at the end.

Responsible AI is designed into the work. We define appropriate data use, access, human oversight, evaluation, escalation, traceability and ownership according to the use case and its risk profile.

WHY UP FRONT

## A control added afterwards doesn’t change how decisions get made.

When boundaries get written after the solution already works, they become a review layer that slows the work down and that people learn to route around. Defined up front, they’re part of how it’s built: the system can’t do what it has no permission to do.

Not every case needs the same thing. An assistant drafting an internal memo and an agent acting on a production system carry different risk profiles, and therefore different controls. What doesn’t change is that they’re decided and written down before anything gets built.

WHAT GETS DEFINED IN EVERY CASE

## Eight decisions, always the same ones.

01

## Data use and access

What information goes in, where it comes from, who can see it and what for. Defined before building, not once it’s already running.

02

## Privacy and security

Handling of personal and sensitive data, encryption, retention and isolation to fit each company’s environment.

03

## Output evaluation

What counts as an acceptable answer and how that gets checked. Without a threshold, “it works well” is an opinion.

04

## Human validation

Where a person reviews, approves or corrects before anything takes effect. The higher the risk, the closer that point sits to the end.

05

## Escalation and exceptions

What happens to the cases that don’t fit: who they reach, how fast, and with what context.

06

## Traceability

What gets recorded about each assisted decision, so it’s possible to reconstruct afterwards why what happened happened.

07

## Named owners

Who answers for the operation and who for the business result. A control with no owner is a document.

08

## Continuous monitoring

How often behavior gets reviewed, who reviews it, and what triggers a change.

ACCOUNTABILITY ISN’T DELEGATED TO THE SYSTEM
