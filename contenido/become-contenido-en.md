THE THESIS

<!-- b1660 -->
## Six stages and five systems.

<!-- b1661 -->
Most AI initiatives do not fail because of the model. They fail because they were built next to the real work: the process stayed the same, nobody owned the outcome, and there was no measure against which to decide whether it was worth keeping.

<!-- b1662 -->
This method exists to give that redesign an order. These are not consulting phases invented to justify a calendar: it is the sequence in which the decisions depend on each other. You cannot choose a capability before you know where the value is, and you cannot measure what was never defined.

<!-- b1663 -->
THE FIVE SYSTEMS

<!-- b1664 -->
## What changes together, not in parallel.

<!-- b1665 -->

<!-- b1666 -->
### People

<!-- b1667 -->
People able to work, decide and collaborate with AI: with their own judgment about what a model can and cannot be asked for, and with ownership of the outcome.

<!-- b1668 -->

<!-- b1670 -->

<!-- b1672 -->

<!-- b1674 -->
### Operations

<!-- b1675 -->
Workflows redesigned to combine automation, agents, human decisions, exceptions and controls in a single process.

## Five principles we do not trade for speed.

<!-- b1713 -->
### Value before technology

<!-- b1714 -->
The conversation does not start with which model to use. It starts with which decision, which process or which experience has to change. The technology is chosen afterwards — which is why it can be changed without redoing the strategy.

<!-- b1715 -->
The team that will operate the capability helps design it. Not for consensus: because the people who run a process are the ones who know its exceptions, and those exceptions decide whether something works in real life.

<!-- b1716 -->
### Adoption by design

<!-- b1717 -->
Adoption is not a communication plan at the end. It is a design decision from the start: what gets embedded in the tool people already use, what gets simplified, and what stops being asked of people at all.

<!-- b1718 -->
### Governance from the start

<!-- b1719 -->
Limits, human oversight, traceability and accountability are defined before anything is built. Adding them later means rebuilding — and in a supervised environment, rebuilding with an auditor watching.

<!-- b1720 -->
### Ownership stays inside

<!-- b1721 -->
When the work ends, the company holds the judgment, the documentation and the people able to operate and evolve what was built. If continuing requires calling us again, the work is not finished.

<!-- b2721 -->
WHY UP FRONT

<!-- b2722 -->
## A control added afterwards doesn’t change how decisions get made.

<!-- b2723 -->
When boundaries get written after the solution already works, they become a review layer that slows the work down and that people learn to route around. Defined up front, they’re part of how it’s built: the system can’t do what it has no permission to do.

<!-- b2724 -->
Not every case needs the same thing. An assistant drafting an internal memo and an agent acting on a production system carry different risk profiles, and therefore different controls. What doesn’t change is that they’re decided and written down before anything gets built.

<!-- b2725 -->

<!-- b2726 -->

<!-- b2727 -->

<!-- b2729 -->

<!-- b2731 -->
## Output evaluation

<!-- b2732 -->

<!-- b2733 -->
04

<!-- b2734 -->
## Human validation

<!-- b2735 -->

<!-- b2736 -->
05

<!-- b2737 -->
## Escalation and exceptions

<!-- b2739 -->
06

<!-- b2740 -->

<!-- b2742 -->
07

<!-- b2744 -->

<!-- b2745 -->
08
